## Third pass: the full pool, 101 cards (Nov 2, 2026)

> Claude's draft at Cam's request, nothing Decided. This is the third pass with both faces; the Oct 31 section further down, also called a third pass, covered permanence faces only. Built from Cam's walkthrough of every effect bucket (design/third-pass-brief.md). Faces drawn Nov 2 on the Card Anatomy canvas (boards titled "Third pass"). Commons 1–40, uncommons 41–68, rares 69–88, fifth-pillar meta cards 89–95, pinnacles 96–100 (the fifth-pillar pinnacle at 100), and card 101 outside the pool (CARD-13).

**How the text reads.** One wording per idea, used the same way on every card:

- **loses its affinities**: counts toward neither world points nor affinity.
- **adjacent**: shares a side in a 2×2; diagonal cards aren't adjacent.
- **remove**: to its owner's void. **return**: to its owner's binder.
- **lone**: alone in its world. **with one affinity**: one ring on the card, an either-or included.
- **a fire permanence**: any permanence showing fire, an either-or included.
- **◯ permanence**: a permanence with one affinity, an either-or included (drawn as a single ring on the card).
- **can't be moved** and **ignores … forces** are the only protection wordings. Nothing in the pool says "can't be removed" on its own permanence.
- **A force has its card's elements.** Gust is an air force because Ledge is air; Hot Spring's Scald is both a fire and a water force. Force tiers follow card rarity: common forces are everyday weather, uncommon ones are severe, rare ones are disasters.
- **"When played"** effects happen once, as the permanence goes down.

**The elemental cycle (Claude's proposal, not adopted).** Each element presses on one other: water douses fire, fire burns earth, earth blocks air, air dries water. At common, removal and "loses its affinities" follow it (fire removes or blanks earth, water fire, earth air, air water); uncommons reach two elements or two cards; rares reach any permanence or a whole world. The same cycle sets which forces a single-affinity common ignores (its predator's: "Ashfield ignores water forces"), which neighbor it turns to its own element ("Lava Field turns adjacent earth to fire"), and which neighbor it can't stand ("Badlands loses its affinities if adjacent to water"). Conversion forces are looser: each element has one that follows the cycle and one that doesn't.

**What's new from the brief.** All 16 three-affinity rares (four with three different elements, twelve two-and-one splits); one cycle of two-of-the-same uncommons; a wildcard uncommon (Wilderness); twelve either-or commons, six of them ignoring the forces of the two elements they lack; one cycle of commons that return to the binder at round end (Fumarole, Spring, Eyrie, Grove); returns only for several cards, a whole world, or your own card as bait; more moves, mostly air; removal climbing by element; Archipelago at 8 water, checked at the start of your turn; Cradle, which wins the contest with 3 of each affinity; Mirage copying every adjacent card with no affinity of its own; Lull at rare; Echo playing any card from your void (Cam's change, Nov 2); Ebb limited to commons; Horizon as a binder discard.

**Gone from the second pass.** Permanence played on another card (Snowfield, Glacier and Salt Flat's old faces), Petrified Forest, "also counts as" forces, Shimmer and Haze, and counting double toward affinity.

**Counts.** 40 commons, 28 uncommons, 20 rares, 4 elemental pinnacles, 7 meta cards and the Gap, plus card 101. Affinities across the 100: fire 33, water 34, air 35, earth 36, plus 6 either-or capsules for each element and one wildcard. Rules text fits in two lines on the card. With Option A (below), 27 forces and Drift run to 8–10 words, shrunk to fit as needed; Archipelago and Cradle keep Cam's wording for their win conditions.

**Keep me honest (CORE-07).** These could be fun for one player and miserable for the other, so they're the first to watch in testing: Great Uplift (Pangaea's force, your worlds ignore all forces for the round), Massif (your worlds ignore common forces, all the time), Calm (no forces for the round, Cam's test of a stronger version), Whiteout (no world grows until your next turn), the four element-wide protections on the two-of-the-same uncommons, and Lull. Each one either lasts a single round or turn, can itself be removed (Thaw removes a force), or applies to both players.

**Changes after review (Nov 2, Cam).** Echo plays any card from your void; Calm lasts the round. Option A: the 23 common forces that aimed at "a [element] permanence" now reach only a permanence with one affinity, and so do the four uncommon removal forces (Scald, Lava Bomb, Hail, Landslide), so a common or uncommon force can't remove, blank, recolour or move a pinnacle or a three-affinity card. "One of yours" now reads "one of your permanences" (Vent, Snowfield, Bluff, Drift, Hanging Valley).

**Changes after the first games (Nov 2, Cam).** The eight common forces that removed or moved a permanence with one affinity now last the round and turn a permanence of any size to the force's element instead (Brushfire: "An earth permanence becomes fire."). The permanence then has a single affinity of the new element, however many it printed: a three-affinity card becomes one fire. Every "becomes" force on one permanence works this way. Nothing at common removes or moves a permanence any more. Flare-up was pointless and now removes a common force. Equinox was far too strong (Cam brought back 17 cards in the third round) and now returns 3 cards from your void.

**Test rule (Nov 2, Cam).** A decisive win: if a player creates a world that scores 3 world points, they win the contest at once. The four test binders below leave out the pinnacles, Origin and the Gap, which are meant for the end game.

**Open.** The cycle above; whether the commons' restriction text ("Loses its affinities if adjacent to water") is worth its words; Horizon's final shape; card 101's force; names that may sit at the wrong tier.

## Second pass: 50 cards plus 5 meta (Nov 1, 2026)

> Claude's draft at Cam's request, nothing Decided: half the pool (30 commons, 13 uncommons, 5 rares, 2 pinnacles) with both faces, plus five weaker fifth-pillar meta cards, and two playtest binders. Text-first: new force faces have placeholder art. Drawn on the [Card Anatomy canvas](https://claude.ai/artifact/5AWUHQhCvrbEw3YSmxbqac) under "Second pass". Rules followed: rules text under 8 words (CARD-23), effects up and restrictions down with rarity (CARD-24), balanced for the likely best case (CARD-25); worlds up to 2×2, scoring one world point per complete set; forces target either player's cards unless they say otherwise; moves and swaps never change a card's owner; "remove" sends a card to its owner's void; a card that "doesn't count" is treated as if it weren't there.

> Revised Nov 1 (Cam): the six dual commons show an either-or mark instead of "Counts as X or Y" text, and Confluence drops its text. On the cards, every affinity sits in a white ring, and an either-or affinity is one capsule holding both symbols, divided by a slash; one ring or one capsule is one affinity, its element chosen at round end for a capsule. Also, a force that lasts the round sits on one card or beside one world and never affects the whole table, so Ash Cloud now hits one card, Blizzard happens once, and Verge plays two cards.

Affinities across the 50: fire 14, water 16, air 15, earth 18, plus 6 either-or capsules and one any. 11 of the 30 commons carry a subtle permanence effect; six carry an either-or capsule, one per pair of elements.

**Choices in this draft:**
- Commons use one simple verb each (play on a card, return, move, shelter) and reach only cards with one tag, so rarer cards are sturdier.
- Uncommons reach any card or shape a whole world; rares remove, split or sweep part of a world; the pinnacle sweeps a whole world. The Black Hole meta card (remove a whole world) sits above that (CARD-12).
- Single-card hits favor big worlds with spare tags; Erosion, Rising Sea and Deluge favor spreading out.
- Force faces look different from permanence: a cooler body, a dashed inner border, an italic name, no tags, and a duration line in place of the tags.

**Revised Nov 1** after Cam locked rules text under 8 words (CARD-23), effects up and restrictions down with rarity (CARD-24), and balancing for the likely best case (CARD-25). Cam's notes: Fog kept; Gale now swaps; Steam Burst reworded and restricted to a touching fire-water pair (placing fire and water diagonally in a 2×2 dodges it); Glacier plays only on earth permanence and turns it to water. Worlds are now capped at 2×2, rectangles only. Conventions in these drafts: forces can target either player's cards unless they say otherwise; "remove" sends a card to its owner's void.

**Open:** whether common forces should stay limited to one-tag cards; every other card's text still needs a pass for the 8-word rule.

## How the sample is built

- **Pairs.** Each card is a landscape (the Host's face) and the force that same place becomes or unleashes (the Guest's face).
- **Rarity by scale.** Commons are local, uncommons regional, rares continental. Pinnacles are planetary and are left out here.
- **Tags on the Host's face only.** Each tag makes the landscape strong or weak against an element. A card's row follows from its tags.
- **One rule for clashes.** When two tags disagree on the same element, the strength wins.
- **Cam's tests for a tag.** It has to be tied to the identity of the card's word and be in his lexicon for landscapes ("a sheltered canyon" works, "an absorbent marsh" doesn't). It shouldn't imply size, since scale already sets rarity. And tags have to be balanced across the pool: the working target is 15 to 20 tags, each on roughly 8 to 15 of the 92 Host faces, which is about 3 to 7 uses in a 40-card sample.
- **Air is the heights.** Earth is mass (the mountain); air is height (the summit, the spire, the ledge), inspired by the Air Nomads in Avatar: The Last Airbender and the floating islands in Avatar.
- **Marks:** \* is a word not on the Shortlist; † is fiction (CARD-21). The air pillar's forces are wind, so its column in the tables is Wind.

## Gaps and open questions

- **Cam's first reactions (Oct 29).** Massive was out: it sounds bad, and it doesn't fit small things like a stone. The first air cards didn't feel like air. Both are addressed in the second pass.
- **Cam on the second pass (Oct 29).** Air is going to need a lot of work: a couple seem fine, most aren't good. Earth, water and fire are more than 50% good, so it's the right track. Fiction is parked for now (CARD-21), so the Floating island is on hold.
- **Earth still beats the most cards, and wind the fewest.** Earth beats 16 and is resisted by 9; wind beats 9 and is resisted by 15. The heights help air hold against earth, but much of the pool is still rock that falls when the ground moves.
- **Fire's landscapes are the weakest.** Fire's natural ground is barren or dry. Fiction could give fire sturdier ground, such as a flame that keeps burning in the rain (CARD-21).
- **Fire is two forces in one column.** Flame and drought both belong to fire, and they don't always hit the same things (a marsh holds against flame but dies in a drought).
- **Five cards have no weakness:** Rainforest, Cove, Bog, Reef and Sea stack. Only raw force clears them. That may be fine, but it's worth testing.
- **The Guest's faces have no tags yet.** Only the landscapes have been tagged.
- **The rarity mix runs a little high.** The sample is 24 common, 12 uncommon and 4 rare. The pool's elemental cards are 60, 20 and 8.
- **Most air words aren't on the Shortlist,** since it had almost no air landscapes. Spark and Steam aren't either.
